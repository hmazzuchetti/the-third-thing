Chapter 1 — The Box

Rascunho. Inspired by the series Dark Matter.

Everything changed the day I walked into that damned box. It was a black, cold box. It didn't shine, it just sat there inside that warehouse where we used to go to get high: me, Bianca, and some "friends". Thing is, I always wanted to impress Bianca, so I did a lot of idiotic stuff to get her attention, and that's where it all began. We were seventeen, eighteen maybe. I should have had some sense back then, but hey, love makes you stupid, and it makes you do crazy things.

Like I said, the warehouse. Abandoned factory, or whatever it used to be, I never paid much attention to the place. Broken windows up high, a concrete floor with oil stains older than us, a smell of rust and wet cardboard. We had a spot near the front, a couple of pallets we'd dragged into a circle, and that was our living room. We had a cooler, I loved beer, and some joints, why not?

We were talking about college, I think. I never wanted to go to college. Loser stuff, I would think. I was finishing a technical course in IT and I wanted to open my own business, computers I guess. I don't quite remember... cleaning, formatting, fixing. Yes, that's it. I believe I was going to call it "Fix'em". Hahaha, funny name. Ironic, if you ask me. We'll get there.

"What about you, John? College?"

"Engineering, I guess," John said. "Pays well."

"You actually fuck with math?" I said. "Sounds crazy. And boring."

John shrugged and opened another beer. He was the kind of guy who already knew what he'd be doing at forty, and I kind of hated him for it, and kind of envied him too. Somewhere out there, I'm pretty sure, there's a version of me who listened to John that night and went to engineering school. I hope he's doing fine. I hope he's bored out of his mind.

Then there was Caio and Letícia, who were always together and never officially together, and who spent most of the night arguing about a song neither of them could remember the name of. That was the group. Five kids on pallets, in the dark, pretending we had it all figured out.

And Bianca.

Bianca sat next to me. She always sat next to me. If I went out for a smoke, she came. If I climbed something stupid, she climbed it after me, usually better. If I said let's go, she was already standing up. She never said much about it, she just came along, every time, like it was the most normal thing in the world.

I thought I was friend-zoned. I was an idiot.

I'd spent two years trying to find the right moment to tell her, and two years deciding that the right moment wasn't now. So instead of talking, I did things. Loud things. Things that made her look at me. That was my whole strategy, and it was a terrible one, and it's the reason I'm telling you this story.

Around midnight I went to take a piss in the back. Nobody went to the back. It was darker there, past a row of rusted machines, and the floor was covered in glass. I used my phone as a flashlight, and that's when I saw it.

The box.

I had seen it before, I think. Or I'd seen a big dark shape back there and never thought about it. But this time I looked. It was taller than me, maybe three meters on each side, perfectly black, no logo, no rust, no graffiti. Everything in that warehouse was tagged, every wall, every machine, and this thing was clean. That should have told me something. It didn't.

And next to it, on the floor, there was a briefcase.

A metal briefcase, the kind you see in movies, with two latches. I remember thinking it was drugs, or money, and that either way it was probably going to get us killed. So obviously I opened it.

Inside, in foam slots, there were vials. Ten, maybe twelve. Small glass vials full of a dark liquid. Not black exactly. When I moved the light over it, it looked like it had depth, like looking down into deep water at night. Like something was in there, far away.

I brought it back to the pallets.

"What the fuck is that?" Caio said.

"Found it next to the box."

"What box?"

So I showed them. All five of us went to the back with our phones up, and we stood in front of that thing like idiots in a museum. John knocked on it. It didn't sound hollow and it didn't sound solid. It didn't sound like anything.

"Somebody's gonna come back for that briefcase," John said. "Put it back, man."

He was right. Of course he was right. John was always right, that's why he went to engineering school.

But Bianca was looking at me. So I took out one of the vials, held it up to the light, and said the dumbest sentence of my life:

"Anyone wanna try?"

Nobody did. Letícia actually took a step back. Caio laughed like I was joking. John just shook his head, already done with me.

I pulled the cap off. It smelled like nothing. Like cold air.

"Don't," John said.

I drank it.

It tasted like metal, and then it tasted like nothing again. I remember waiting for something to happen and feeling kind of disappointed that nothing did. I wiped my mouth and grinned at them like I'd just won something.

Then Bianca took a vial out of the briefcase.

"Bia, what are you doing?" Letícia said.

She didn't answer. She just looked at me, opened it and drank it, the same way she climbed things after me, like it was the most normal thing in the world. And I stood there thinking, she's so cool, she's such a good friend.

Like I said. An idiot.

At first it was just a little dizzy. A soft, warm spin, like the first beer on an empty stomach. Bianca laughed and grabbed my arm to keep her balance, and I remember that more than anything else from that night, her hand on my arm.

"It's just some weird shit," I said. "It's nothing."

The others went back to the pallets. They were scared, and pissed at me, and they didn't want to be anywhere near that box anymore. But me and Bianca stayed. We wandered around the back of the warehouse, high as kites, laughing at nothing, touching the old machines, reading graffiti out loud in stupid voices.

And somehow, every time we turned around, we were back in front of the box.

The third time, I noticed there was a door on it. A thin line in the black, the size of a person. I swear it wasn't there before. I put my hand on it, and it was cold, colder than anything should be in September, and it moved. Just a little. Like it had been waiting for me to push.

"You're not going in there," Bianca said.

"I'm just gonna look."

"Then I'm looking too."

Of course she was.

I pushed the door. It opened without making a sound, and inside there was only dark. Not the dark of a room with the lights off. A dark that went on.

I looked back at her. She was smiling, a little scared, a little high, a hundred percent coming with me.

We stepped in together, and the door closed behind us.
